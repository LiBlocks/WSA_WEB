FAMMOK: Planetary Exploration Program

FAMMOK is WSA's lander and planetary-mission development program. Mission numbers indicate progression in capability and ambition, not merely cosmetic changes. Each FAMMOK mission builds on previous experience and explores new operational challenges.

FAMMOK-1: Mun Capability Baseline

▼

Mission Objective: Establish a Mun-capable lander and develop foundational capability for planetary landing operations.

The detailed historical record for FAMMOK-1 is incomplete in the current archive. This mission represents the baseline lander capability that underpins subsequent FAMMOK programs. Verified mission details, vehicle specifications, landing location, and engineering results will be added when documented records are available.

Significance: FAMMOK-1 established that WSA could successfully land on and return from the Mun. This capability enabled all subsequent planetary missions.

FAMMOK-2: Minmus & Duna Exploration

▼

Mission Objective: Extended operations to Minmus and Duna, advancing planetary exploration capability and testing long-range mission architecture.

Campaign Performance Record

The following figures are recorded from FAMMOK-2 campaign logs and represent approximate performance characteristics across the mission profile:

Pre-Burn (Injection Phase): ~6,000 m/s delta-v
Lander Capability: ~3,690 m/s delta-v (surface-to-orbit)
Duna Orbit Achieved: Ap ~440 km, Pe ~260 km
Duna Orbit Phase Delta-v: ~3,100 m/s
Duna Landing Attempt: ~2,300 m/s remaining at surface contact

Note: These are approximate figures from campaign records. They represent various stages of the mission and should not be interpreted as specifications for a single vehicle configuration. Different stages and vehicles may have contributed different values.

Key Events:

  • Duna Orbit Achievement: Orbital insertion successful around Duna with established elliptical orbit. Navigation and long-range transfer proved viable.
  • Entry & Landing Attempt: Duna landing sequence initiated. Vehicle descended toward surface with parachute deployment.
  • Parachute Issue: Parachute malfunction or performance problem during Duna descent. Lander reached surface with high residual velocity (~2,300 m/s).
  • Survivability: Lander survived surface contact despite high velocity. Vehicle integrity preserved for recovery operations.

Engineering Lesson: Parachute design and deployment reliability are critical for planetary landing. Duna's atmosphere requires careful parachute sizing—too small and the vehicle arrives too fast, too large and staging becomes problematic. FAMMOK-2's parachute issue drove subsequent design improvements and backup-system planning.

FAMMOK-3: Rescue & Deep-Space Operations

▼

Mission Objective: Future rescue and high-capacity deep-space mission, leveraging improved launch capability and addressing contingency scenarios.

Current Status: Planned / In Planning Phase. Not yet flown.

FAMMOK-3 represents the next evolution of WSA's planetary-exploration capability. The mission architecture incorporates several advances:

  • Launch Vehicle: Intended to use Aegis-1 Super Heavy heavy-lift configuration, providing greater payload capacity than previous missions.
  • Rescue Capability: Mission designed to address contingency scenarios and support rescue operations for stranded vehicles or crew from earlier missions.
  • Improved Parachute System: Incorporates lessons from FAMMOK-2 parachute issues. Redundant and improved descent systems planned.
  • Extended Mission Duration: Designed for more ambitious surface operations and longer orbital phases than FAMMOK-2.
  • Return Architecture: Full return vehicle and recovery capability planned, not just lander descent.

Note: FAMMOK-3 details and architecture remain under development. Specific objectives, destination, and timeline will be updated as planning progresses.

Project Silver Bridge

Silver Bridge was an experimental, one-off mission designed to test surface operations on the Mun and evaluate return-and-dock procedures with KGOSS orbital station.

Mission Concept & Objectives

▼

Primary Goals:

  • Travel to the Mun and achieve lunar orbit
  • Undock from orbit to descend to surface
  • Conduct surface operations: drilling and ISRU-related experiments
  • Return to Munar orbit for rendezvous
  • Execute docking with KGOSS for return to Kerbin
  • Support experimental relay operations

Mission Status: Partially successful. Critical docking-compatibility issue prevented completion of planned return sequence.

Critical Issue: Docking Port Mismatch

▼

The Problem:

Silver Bridge's return vehicle possessed a docking port size that did not match KGOSS's available docking ports. The incompatibility was discovered after the lander returned to Munar orbit, making the planned docking impossible.

Consequences: The vehicle could not dock with KGOSS as intended. This forced improvised recovery procedures and created a contingency scenario requiring coordinated rescue planning.

Critical Lesson: Docking compatibility cannot be assumed. All vehicles operating in the same mission architecture must have verified compatible docking ports. This failure drove subsequent WSA policy requiring formal compatibility reviews before any crewed or complex multi-vehicle missions. Silver Bridge demonstrates why compatible docking hardware, return options, and contingency rescue planning are not optional extras—they are essential mission-success requirements.

Near-Stranding in Kerbin Orbit

▼

Secondary Issue: After the Munar-phase docking incompatibility became apparent, the return vehicle faced a contingency situation in low Kerbin orbit.

Unable to dock with KGOSS as planned, the vehicle required alternative recovery procedures. WSA coordinated rescue and recovery operations to retrieve the stranded vehicle and crew.

Resolution: Rescue mission successfully recovered the vehicle and crew from Kerbin orbit. This operation demonstrated WSA's contingency-planning capability and the importance of maintaining rescue readiness for unexpected situations.

Significance & Legacy

▼

Silver Bridge is recorded not as a failure, but as a valuable and honest example of mission planning challenges:

  • Documented Lesson: Incompatibility discovery led to formal docking-compatibility procedures and design reviews.
  • Rescue Capability Proof: WSA's ability to execute unexpected rescue operations was validated and documented.
  • Contingency Planning: Demonstrated that WSA systems and procedures could handle unexpected situations without total mission loss.
  • Operational Improvement: Incident drove improvements in vehicle design reviews, mission planning, and compatibility validation.

This mission is a model of why WSA documents failures and problems—they drive real engineering improvements and inform future decision-making.
