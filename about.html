About Woody Space Agency

A small, independent aerospace organization pursuing ambitious spaceflight through incremental engineering and reusable systems.

Engineering Philosophy

WSA's core principle is to develop engineering solutions step-by-step, learning from each test and iteration rather than immediately reaching for third-party automation. This approach demands patience, rigorous documentation, honest failure analysis, and continuous improvement.

Incremental Development

Systems are built through successive refinement. Each test flight, mission, or experiment teaches something about the vehicle, the control system, or the mission profile. Failures are documented as learning opportunities, not setbacks.

Homemade Guidance & Control

Rather than delegating all autonomous tasks to external mods, WSA develops custom flight controllers and guidance systems (like AutoLand) to understand how spacecraft actually behave and respond to control inputs.

Reusable-First Design

Boosters are designed for recovery, refurbishment, and reuse. This drives engineering decisions about structure, landing systems, and operational procedures that would not exist in expendable designs.

Reliable Operations

WSA prioritizes systems that can repeat their performance consistently. A vehicle that lands safely once proves a concept; a vehicle that lands safely 47+ times proves a capability.

Mission-Focused

Development is driven by real mission objectives: reaching the Mun, assembling an orbital station, recovering boosters, supporting crew transport. Technology is built to serve these goals.

Documented Learning

Flight records, engineering decisions, problems, and solutions are documented. This archive allows WSA to review progress, avoid repeating mistakes, and make informed decisions about future programs.

Core Interests

WSA's engineering work focuses on these interconnected technical areas:

Reusable Booster Development & Recovery

Designing boosters that survive descent, landing, and recovery. This includes structural design for reuse, thermal management, landing-gear systems, and operational procedures for refurbishment.

Autonomous Descent, Guidance & Landing Control

Building guidance systems that predict trajectories, manage throttle, and guide vehicles to precision landings. AutoLand represents the flagship effort in this area—a custom kOS system for reliable booster recovery.

Orbital Station Construction & Logistics

Assembling modular orbital infrastructure (KGOSS), managing docking, transferring fuel, and supporting sustained space operations. Station work drives requirements for reliable rendezvous and crew transportation.

Crew Transport, Rendezvous, Docking & Rescue

Developing systems for human spaceflight: reliable crew capsules, safe entry and recovery, rendezvous and docking with orbital stations, and rescue capability for contingency scenarios.

Aircraft & Spaceplane Experimentation

Testing flight-control concepts, atmospheric performance, high-speed flight, and reusable transportation. Programs like Swift-1X and SSTO development explore aerodynamic and orbital-transition challenges.

Mun, Minmus, Duna & Planetary Exploration

Extending mission capability beyond Kerbin orbit. FAMMOK develops lander systems, precision-landing guidance, entry profiles, and return capability for distant destinations.

Mission Planning & Operations

Understanding orbital mechanics, planning transfer trajectories, managing consumables, coordinating multi-vehicle operations, and preparing contingency responses for unexpected situations.

What WSA Is Not

To be clear about WSA's scope and nature:

  • Not a real aerospace corporation. WSA is a fictional organization developed within Kerbal Space Program. Its achievements exist in simulation.
  • Not a commercial spaceflight company. WSA pursues engineering and exploration, not profit or mass transport.
  • Not a government agency. WSA operates as an independent organization focused on research and development.
  • Not an autopilot mod. AutoLand is a custom-written kOS script, not a complete replacement for pilot decision-making.
  • Not a single monolithic system. WSA is a collection of programs, vehicles, and experimental efforts that evolve independently.

Organization & Structure

WSA operates through several interconnected program areas, each pursuing specific engineering goals while contributing to the broader mission architecture:

Aegis Program

Development and operation of the Aegis reusable booster family. Includes multiple configurations, autonomous landing research, and recovery operations. Serves as the foundation for many WSA missions.

Lyra Program

Next-generation reusable launcher family. Represents a new direction in vehicle design with focus on capacity, reliability, and supporting more ambitious mission architectures like KGOSS expansion.

AutoLand Engineering

Autonomous guidance and control system development. Spans all booster families and represents WSA's commitment to understanding how to reliably land spacecraft without human intervention.

KGOSS Orbital Operations

Construction and operation of the KGOSS orbital station. Drives requirements for crew transport, logistics, docking systems, and rescue capability. Central hub for sustained space operations.

FAMMOK Exploration Program

Planetary lander development and deep-space mission planning. Focuses on Mun, Minmus, and Duna capabilities, including precision landing and return vehicle development.

Aircraft & Flight Systems

Atmospheric and spaceplane research. Includes Swift-1X test aircraft and SSTO development. Explores flight control, aerodynamics, and reusable orbital transportation concepts.
