KGOSS Station

WSA's orbital infrastructure hub. Central assembly platform for sustained space operations, crew logistics, and future mission expansion.

Station Role: KGOSS is the primary orbital hub for crew transport, logistics, fuel transfer, module assembly, and supporting future deep-space missions.

Operational Status: Active. Station supports ongoing logistics missions, crew operations, and infrastructure expansion.

KGOSS-1: Current Station Configuration

KGOSS-1 is the active orbital station. It operates in a working orbit of approximately 200 km altitude, with higher orbits around 250 km considered as operational alternatives.

Station Purpose & Mission

▼

KGOSS provides essential infrastructure for:

  • Crew Operations: Crewed missions arrive at KGOSS for crew rotation, rest, and operations planning.
  • Logistics Hub: Supply missions dock to transfer cargo, fuel, water, and consumables.
  • Fuel Transfer: Propellant is transferred between vehicles and stored on the station for future missions.
  • Orbital Assembly: Station modules are docked and integrated on-orbit, enabling construction that could not be launched fully assembled.
  • Scientific Operations: Research platforms and experiments operate from the station.
  • Mission Planning Base: Deep-space missions may be planned and staged from KGOSS.
  • Rescue Operations: Station serves as refuge and rescue staging point for contingency scenarios.

Known Modules & Components

▼

KGOSS is a modular station with the following documented components:

Seraph SPC-1 "Haven" Rescue & Return Craft

A Dragon-like capsule for crew rescue and return to Kerbin. Provides emergency return capability and crew transport redundancy.

Role: Crewed return and rescue vehicle. Docked at station.
Centrifuge Module

Large rotating module providing artificial gravity research capability. Approximately 43 tonnes of mass and rotational infrastructure.

Purpose: Scientific research and crew-health research in simulated gravity.
Three Attached Cabins

Crew quarters and operational modules providing living space, workstations, and storage.

Capacity: Support for visiting crew and permanent station personnel.
Planned Fuel Tanks (Proposed Expansion)

Two matching fuel tanks (approximately 84 tonnes each) planned for symmetrical expansion and increased propellant storage.

Status: Designed but not yet launched. Planned for future logistics missions.

Station Mass Note: Current configuration (without planned fuel tanks) represents a significant orbital structure. Future expansion will increase station capability and propellant reserves.

Docking & Rendezvous Operations

▼

KGOSS operations depend on successful rendezvous and docking:

  • Docking Ports: Multiple docking ports support simultaneous crew return capsules, logistics vehicles, and module expansion.
  • Port Compatibility: All WSA vehicles must have compatible docking systems. Project Silver Bridge's docking failure led to strict compatibility requirements.
  • Approach Procedures: Rendezvous requires precise orbital maneuvering, careful approach control, and automated docking procedures.
  • Undocking: Return vehicles undock for the return journey to Kerbin. This procedure must be choreographed to avoid collision.
  • Contingency Planning: Station must maintain rescue capability for unexpected docking or crew situations.

Fuel & Propellant Operations

▼

KGOSS supports critical propellant management:

  • Fuel Transfer Capability: Propellant can be transferred between docked vehicles and station storage.
  • In-Orbit Refueling: Visiting spacecraft can refuel at KGOSS, extending their range and enabling longer missions.
  • Storage Capacity: Current storage supports regular logistics operations. Planned fuel tanks would significantly increase reserve capacity.
  • Safety Procedures: Propellant transfer requires careful pressure management, isolation procedures, and contingency response for leaks or spills.
  • Future Expansion: Advanced transfer systems and boiloff management may evolve with station expansion.

Station Assembly & Evolution

KGOSS was not launched as a complete vehicle. It was assembled in orbit through multiple logistics missions:

Assembly Approach

▼

Building a station on-orbit requires solving multiple challenges that differ from ground-based assembly:

  • Individual Module Launches: Each station module was launched separately and docked on-orbit rather than assembled on the ground.
  • Rendezvous Skill: Assembly required reliable rendezvous and docking procedures. Multiple vehicles had to meet in orbit precisely.
  • Staging & Docking Port Compatibility: Every module required compatible docking hardware. Project Silver Bridge's failure demonstrated why compatibility matters.
  • Orbital Mechanics: Each module had to be launched into compatible orbits and maneuvered to rendezvous with the growing station.
  • Propellant & Logistics: Ongoing station operations require logistics support (fuel, water, supplies) to remain operational.
  • Station-Keeping: The station requires periodic orbital reboosts to maintain altitude as atmospheric drag gradually lowers its orbit.

Key Insight: Launching a complete space station in one piece is not feasible. Modular assembly on-orbit is the only practical approach for large infrastructure. This requires confident spacecraft handling, reliable docking, and proven rendezvous procedures.

Future Expansion Plans

▼

KGOSS will continue to grow as WSA's capabilities expand:

  • Fuel Tank Modules: Two planned fuel tanks (~84 tonnes each) will increase propellant reserves and refueling capacity.
  • Additional Cabins: More crew quarters and laboratory modules to support expanded crew and research.
  • Power Generation: Solar arrays or other power systems to support growing station load.
  • Advanced Docking: Additional docking ports and/or more sophisticated docking systems for higher-traffic operations.
  • Deorbit Infrastructure: KGOSS-3 future work on deorbit-safety systems and controlled end-of-life procedures (see below).

Station expansion will be coordinated with growth in KGOSS-capable launch vehicles, particularly Lyra family development and expansion.

KGOSS-3: Future Deorbit-Safety Research

KGOSS-3 is a future research direction focused on safe end-of-life procedures for large orbital structures.

Deorbit Safety Program

▼

Current Status: Planned future research direction. Not yet implemented.

As orbital infrastructure grows, questions about end-of-life operations become important. KGOSS-3 will research how to safely deorbit large structures to avoid creating debris or threatening ground infrastructure.

Research Areas:

  • Controlled Deorbit Procedures: How to manage the controlled reentry of large structures.
  • Debris Minimization: Engineering approaches to ensure that deorbited structures break up safely during reentry rather than creating ground hazards.
  • Staging Separation: Techniques for separating components to distribute reentry loads.
  • Contingency Procedures: What to do if deorbit systems fail or require alternative approaches.

This research becomes relevant as orbital infrastructure becomes larger and more complex. It represents long-term sustainability planning for orbital operations.

Orbital Operations & Infrastructure

KGOSS represents WSA's first major permanent orbital structure. Future stations, fuel depots, and orbital bases will build on lessons learned from KGOSS operations.

Station Operations & Challenges

Operating a space station involves ongoing engineering challenges:

Orbital Decay & Reboosts

Atmospheric drag gradually lowers the station's orbit. Periodic reboost missions use visiting spacecraft to maintain altitude. This is an ongoing operational requirement.

Micrometeorite & Debris Protection

Station must withstand impacts from orbital debris and micrometeorites. Design includes shielding and structural redundancy to prevent catastrophic damage.

Thermal Management

Radiators manage heat from electrical systems and crew activities. Orbital thermal environment (sun-to-shade transitions every 45 minutes) creates challenging conditions.

Life Support & Consumables

Crew requires regular delivery of oxygen, water, food, and other consumables. Station life-support must be robust and fail-safe for crew safety.

Power Generation & Storage

Solar arrays provide primary power. Battery systems handle eclipse periods (nighttime passes). Power management must support growing station load and future expansion.

Communication & Telemetry

Station must maintain continuous communication with ground control. Telemetry systems monitor all station systems and provide data for mission planning.
