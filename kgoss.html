KGOSS Station

WSA's orbital station and assembly hub, built in Kerbin orbit from separately launched modules.

Status: In service. KGOSS-1 orbits Kerbin at about 200 × 204 km with 38 crew aboard in WSA's current save.

Docked: the Seraph SPC-1 "Haven" rescue capsule, a centrifuge module of about 43 tonnes, three cabins and a fuel module of about 84 tonnes.

KGOSS-1: Current Configuration

The working orbit was discussed at about 200 km, with about 250 km considered. In the current save, which uses the stock-scale Kerbol system, KGOSS-1 is at about 200 × 204 km. When WSA tried KSRSS Reborn, which rescales the system, the old working altitude turned out to be inside the atmosphere.

Modules and Docked Craft

Centrifuge Module

Three cabins attached to the station.

Fuel Modules

The first fuel module, about 84 tonnes, is docked. A second matching tank is planned so the station is symmetrical.

Status: One docked, one planned.

How KGOSS-1 Was Built

KGOSS was not launched as one vehicle. Each piece was launched on its own, brought up to the station's orbit and docked there.

Assembly Flights

▼
  • Core: the station core was launched first and released in a 200 km orbit.
  • Habitation module: the KGOSS-1A habitation module was flown up to the core.
  • Centrifuge: launched as "Centri KGOSS-1", it met the station and docked.
  • Fuel module: "KGOSS-1 Fuel Mdl Mk1" rendezvoused with the station and docked.
  • Seraph SPC-1 "Haven": flew up with three crew and docked as the rescue capsule.

The order above follows the save records; the number of approaches each module needed is not documented.

Visiting Craft and Lessons

▼
  • Mk2 SSTO: a single-stage spaceplane reached the station's orbit, but it had never docked before and the risk of hitting the centrifuge was too great, so the docking was aborted. It landed safely back at KSC.
  • ISRU module: its first docking attempt was called off because it lacked the RCS to control the docking reliably, and it came back to a safe splashdown on Kerbin. It will be docked later.
  • Project Silver Bridge: after landing on the Mun, mining and docking back in Munar orbit, its command module and lander were meant to dock at KGOSS-1 together, but their docking ports did not match the station's. Docking compatibility cannot be assumed.

Future Expansion

▼
  • ISRU module: a second docking attempt for the KGOSS-1 ISRU module.
  • Second fuel tank: a tank matching the docked fuel module, to make the station symmetrical.
  • Lyra: the larger Lyra boosters are intended for future KGOSS construction.
  • KGOSS-3: future research into deorbit safety (below).

KGOSS-3: Future Deorbit-Safety Research

KGOSS-3 is a planned research direction on how large orbital structures can be brought down safely at the end of their lives. It has not started yet.
